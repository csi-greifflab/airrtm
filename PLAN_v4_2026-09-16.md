# Why AIRRTM loses to Fisher, and why `tm` looks flat

## Context

Ten days of Emerson runs have plateaued at held-out `theta_features` ROC-AUC **0.740**
against a Fisher-exact burden baseline of **0.874**, with the TM loss apparently pinned at
its uniform baseline. ~30 configs across encoder width, head count, pooling, warm-start,
topic count, coefficients and seed all land in a 0.63–0.74 band.

This plan revises an earlier draft of mine. Two of its four claims did not survive checking
against the run logs on disk, and the corrections are recorded below rather than quietly
dropped, because two of them invalidate conclusions currently written into FINDINGS.md.

Scope: stay recognisably AIRRTM (topics, Θ, φ, VAE); internal reformulation is fair game.
Priority: held-out repertoire AUC first, sequence attribution second.

---

## Cross-cutting correction: most of the leaderboard is noise

The test split is **70 positive / 82 negative** (152 repertoires). Hanley–McNeil standard
error at AUC ≈ 0.74 is **±0.041**. So:

- The encoder sweep (0.740 / 0.677 / 0.635 / 0.658 for 8 heads / 2 heads / 4 heads·dim128 /
  dim32) spans ~2.5 SE across single runs. **"Wider is worse" and "head count is the lever"
  are not established.**
- 0.740 is the maximum over dozens of configs, so winner's-curse inflation of 1–2 SE puts
  its true value plausibly at ~0.67–0.70 — which also means the real gap to Fisher may be
  larger than 0.134, not smaller.
- FINDINGS.md already measured seed-alone spread of 0.572–0.740 on one unchanged config.
  That is the same statement arrived at empirically.

**Rule for everything below: no single-run comparison under ~0.10 AUC counts. Three seeds
minimum, or don't run it.**

## Diagnosis

### 1. `tm` is not flat — it is undertrained, and the sweep confounded N with budget

**I had this wrong, and so does FINDINGS.md.** The quoted "N=606 → 0.007 nats" is
`overfit_amortized_full`. The matched `theta_mode: free` run at N=606 is
**`overfit_free_full`, which reached 0.0324 nats — 4.6× higher — and was still climbing
monotonically at epoch 446** (0.0058 @39 → 0.0160 @79 → 0.0254 @239 → 0.0306 @439). The
claim "the TM loss essentially never leaves 10.397" is not what the log says.

The scaling sweep was also not "identical config, only `--repertoire_slice` varied".
`config_overfit_free_full.yaml` runs 604 steps/epoch for 447 epochs = **270k steps**,
against 17–21k for every other point. And because steps/epoch and epoch count were held
fixed while N varied, **steps-per-repertoire ∝ 1/N by construction** — the sweep varied
optimisation budget and repertoire count together and could never separate them.

Re-index the same runs by gradient steps per repertoire (= steps × 4 / N):

| steps/rep | 300 (N=256) | 347 (N=606 @ep87) | 600 (N=128) | 1300 (N=64) | 1782 (N=606 @ep447) | 4350 (N=16) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| gain (nats) | 0.0132 | 0.0166 | 0.0262 | 0.0618 | 0.0324 | 0.1593 |
| gain ÷ steps-per-rep | 4.4e-5 | 4.8e-5 | 4.4e-5 | 4.8e-5 | 1.8e-5 | 3.7e-5 |

Five of six sit at **4.4e-5 ± 12%**, including the N=606 point. The sixth is the only run
trained ~5× past the others, i.e. saturating. At matched budget, **N=606 outperforms N=256
by 1.26×** — the opposite direction from any capacity story.

My earlier "gain ≈ 0.11·T/N rank law" is withdrawn. Two reasons it was wrong:

- T = 30 in every run, so dividing by `T/N` is dividing by a constant. It encoded no
  evidence about T at all; it was `gain ∝ 1/N` wearing a hat.
- **The rank constraint is not binding, by ~200×.** `_tm_log_likelihood_per_sequence`
  (`composite_loss.py:317-362`) builds `cross_RS` over the **4 repertoires in the batch**
  and normalises per row. Θ never has to separate 606 repertoires — only 4. Even under a
  hard 30-cluster partition of 606 repertoires, ~82% of random quadruples fall in 4
  distinct clusters, so the rank-30 ceiling at N=606 is ~1.1 nats, not 0.005.

There is one genuine loose thread: `split_sequences` (`utils/loading.py:73-92`) holds out
10% of each repertoire's *distinct clonotypes*, so val gain contains zero memorisation —
yet N=16 val gain is 0.159, **3× above the 0.052-nat V/J/length "ceiling"**. That ceiling
must be a large underestimate (it ignores CDR3 composition entirely and was not measured
under this sampling distribution or the K=4 in-batch protocol). It should not be quoted as
a ceiling again until recomputed under the matching protocol.

### 2. Against Fisher: no free per-item parameter — but the signal may be partly smooth

φ is `Linear(z_sample(64) ++ V_emb(8) ++ J_emb(8) → T)` — a rank-30 linear readout of a
smooth 96-d code from a VAE whose KL term actively pulls `z` toward N(0, I). **There is no
parameter anywhere in the model attached to a specific clonotype**, whereas a classical
topic model's `β ∈ R^{T×V}` is exactly that, and Fisher is 3.5M independent per-clonotype
tests.

This remains the best explanation for the one high-powered negative result in the corpus:
**per-sequence attribution is at chance** — PR-AUC 1.11× for φ, 0.94× for attention×value,
precision@100 exactly **0** in every checkpoint tested. That is not a ±0.041 kind of
result.

**Hedged per your point:** "the signal is discrete, so smoothness is anti-aligned" is
probably too strong. HLA-restricted CMV-reactive TCRs plausibly share real motif structure,
so a sequence model *could* in principle find smooth patterns that Fisher's exact-match
selection cannot express — and if so, AIRRTM has headroom Fisher does not. Two corrections
that follow:

- The encoder sweep does **not** support "capacity is irrelevant" (see the ±0.041 section).
  A transformer can memorise 528 strings; what is missing is not expressive power but
  (a) the 30-dim bottleneck, (b) KL pressure on `z`, and (c) any *training signal* asking
  for it — `_tm_loss` is completely **label-blind**, so it spends capacity on bulk
  composition.
- This turns into an empirical question with a cheap answer, not an argument: does a *free*
  per-clonotype table beat a smooth φ on the same objective? Experiment 3 measures it. If
  smooth φ matches the free table, your reading is right and the model's ceiling is the
  motif structure, not the vocabulary.

**The public-clonotype restriction is sound and is not circular.**
`BurdenScoreClassifier.fit` (`baseline.py:66-99`) filters `totals >= min_repertoires`
(default 4) *before* testing, so the entire 0.874 signal lives inside the public subset by
construction. Restricting a free embedding to clonotypes seen in ≥4 **training** repertoires
is a label-free prefilter that loses no signal and cuts the vocabulary from 3.5M to ~1e5.

### 3. Abundance is applied twice, and Θ effectively reads ~2–25 clonotypes

Fisher scores `hits / n_distinct_clonotypes` — uniform over all ~143k–300k distinct
clonotypes, abundance ignored (`baseline.py:25-46` runs `np.unique`).

The model applies abundance **twice**:

1. `_sample_indices` (`training/train.py:567-593`) draws 8192 indices
   `torch.multinomial(p ∝ duplicate_count, replacement=True)`.
2. `_Columns.add` (`train.py:556-557`) then collects `dataset.weights[indices]` *regardless
   of how those indices were sampled*, and those weights reach `_pool_topic_logits`
   (`airrtm_model.py:415-431`) where they become `+log(count)` inside `_grouped_softmax`, or
   a multiplicative weight in `_grouped_mean`.

Effective pooling weight on a clonotype is therefore **∝ count²**. Measured effective sample
size (`1/Σq²`) of that count² distribution on five random repertoires: **10.0, 1.7, 24.8,
3.3, 11.4**. Θ — the only thing `label_input="repertoire"` reads and the only thing
`theta_features` evaluates — is determined by roughly **2 to 25 clonotypes per repertoire**.
With `theta_pooling: "mean"` that is hard-coded and unlearnable; with attention the network
must learn a `−log(count) ≈ −8` correction just to reach neutral.

This is the single most consequential defect found, and it contaminates every run in
FINDINGS.md.

Two related points:

- **Uniform-vs-abundance sampling is not itself a coverage lever.** Measured distinct
  clonotypes drawn in 8192: abundance-weighted 7,372–7,873; uniform 7,846–8,081. A 2–6%
  difference. The value of your proposal is not coverage — it is that it stops abundance
  entering twice and puts it where it belongs.
- **The MIL pooling has no witness-isolating operator at the τ actually used.**
  `_label_loss` pools `(logsumexp(τ·l) − log n)/τ` with `tau: 1.0` (`tau_start: 0.1`) over
  n = 8192. At τ=1 a witness needs a logit ~`ln 8192` = 9 above the bulk to control the bag;
  at τ=0.1, ~90. For `label_input="sequence"` the "smooth max" is de facto a **mean**, and
  1.8 witnesses in 8192 move it by 2e-4·Δ.

There is a possible hard ceiling here that should be measured before anything else is built:
a Monte-Carlo of the oracle Fisher score under bag-size subsampling suggests AUC ≈ **0.69**
from a single 8192 bag and ≈ **0.82** at the eval-time 4×16384. If that holds, 0.740 is
already at the *sampling* ceiling and no modelling change reading an 8192 bag can beat it.
Experiment 2 settles this in minutes on CPU.

### 4. v1's TM term is supervised; v3's is label-blind — and v1's config has never run

This is stronger than I first put it. `archive/utils.py:155-170` (`generate_random_sample_ids`)
duplicates every sequence and pairs the copy with a repertoire id **of the opposite class**,
target 0. v1's "topic model" is a **supervised, sequence-level, class-contrastive
objective** — which is exactly why its per-sequence precision@k works. v3's `_tm_loss`
batch-softmax is completely label-blind. These are different objectives, not different
hyperparameters of one objective.

Also confirmed:

- v1 has no softmax on Θ (`archive/model.py:223`, commented out), `entropy_regularizer_coef
  = 0.0`, 8 topics (4+4), latent 70, lr 1e-3, and its label head reads the free row directly
  (`:263`). `theta_entropy_coef` / `topic_usage_coef` exist **only** because of the simplex
  this codebase added — and FINDINGS.md separately measured that collapse does not predict
  AUC, i.e. the fight they were added to win doesn't correlate with the outcome.
- The synthetic collapse signature is the *predicted* signature of those regularisers, not a
  mystery: `theta_entropy_coef: 0.5` minimises mean per-repertoire entropy while
  `topic_usage_coef: 5.0` pushes on the mean; H_theta→0 **with** H_usage→0 is "every Θ
  one-hot on the same topic", after which a `label_input="repertoire"` head reads a constant
  and returns a constant logit — **exactly 0.5** on a 50/50 split.
- The real v1 config has never run: the guard at `airrtm_model.py:131-136` rejects
  `label_input="repertoire"` + `theta_mode="free"`, so the 2026-09-14 "v1-style" runs used
  `theta_mode="amortized"` instead.
- Those runs were additionally dead for a mechanical reason: `_tm_loss_raw_bce`
  (`composite_loss.py:389-391`) `clamp`s the raw dot product into `(eps, 1−eps)` before
  `binary_cross_entropy`, and **`clamp` has zero gradient outside the range**. With an
  unbounded amortized pooled dot product most sequences contribute no gradient at all. v1
  survived the same clipping only because a freshly-initialised Keras `Embedding` keeps the
  dot product near 0. "v1-style stayed flat for 170 epochs" licenses no conclusion about v1.

**One overreach of mine to retract:** "the synthetic failure blocks every Emerson
conclusion" is too strong. S1/S2 have 100 repertoires, all `split=train`, no test set — so
`label_acc` there is a *training* metric and the task is memorising 100 labels, which a free
lookup table plus a logistic head does trivially. It is a necessary sanity gate, not an
epistemic blocker.

## Answering your question: what should Θ be?

**Recommendation: amortized, unnormalised, pooled as an unweighted mean over distinct
clonotypes, with both entropy regularisers off.** Reasoning, in order of confidence:

1. **Amortized, not free.** Non-negotiable given the evaluation protocol — `theta_features`
   scores held-out repertoires the model never saw, and a free embedding has no row for
   them. (`theta_mode="both"` is *not* the compromise it looks like: 
   `infer_repertoire_topic_proportions` drops the free row entirely at inference, so the
   label head would train on `softmax(amortized + free)` and be evaluated on
   `softmax(amortized)` — a train/eval shift on its own input, on top of the free residual
   being the lowest-loss path and starving the amortized one. Usable only with the residual
   masked with probability p each step, or annealed to zero, plus L2. Not worth it yet.)
2. **Unweighted mean over distinct clonotypes** — this is the §3 fix, and it makes Θ
   estimate the same statistic Fisher's burden score is.
3. **Drop the simplex, or at least the entropy pair.** The softmax is what created the two
   degenerate attractors; the entropy pair exists only to fight them; and collapse was
   measured not to predict AUC. Since the TM loss normalises explicitly over a candidate
   pool, the generative reading survives without the simplex — it becomes an energy-based
   model over sequences, which is what the batch-softmax already is. Replace the entropy
   terms with a mild L2 on Θ (v1's `topic_proportions_l2_coef = 1e-4`).

Points 2 and 3 are independent, so this is a clean 2×2 (experiment 6). Do not run it before
experiment 1, because the double-weighting fix changes what every other result means.

## Ranked experiments

**Capacity:** 7 of 8 GPUs are idle (only GPU 6 busy). Experiments 1, 2, 4, 5 can start
immediately; 3 needs building.

### 1. Fix the double abundance weighting, and move the weights into the loss — *your point 3*

The highest-value change in this document, and the cheapest. Currently abundance is sampled
on *and* pooled on. The design:

- **Sampling:** `abundance_weighted_sampling: false` → `_sample_indices` already takes a
  `randperm` branch, i.e. uniform over **distinct clonotypes, without replacement**. Exactly
  what you asked for, no code change.
- **Θ pooling:** unweighted. Stop `_Columns.add` (`train.py:556-557`) from collecting
  `dataset.weights[indices]` for the Θ path, or neutralise `log_weights_S` in
  `_pool_topic_logits` (`airrtm_model.py:415-431`).
- **Weights in the loss instead:** the true `p(s|r)` *is* abundance-proportional, so the
  principled home for the counts is a per-sequence weight on the **TM likelihood** and on
  **reconstruction** — not on Θ, and not on the MIL label pooling (which should mirror
  Fisher's dedup'd burden score).
- **Detail to get right, with a test:** the TM partition function's candidate pool is now
  uniform over distinct clonotypes while the numerator is abundance-weighted, making it an
  importance-weighted estimator. Add a unit test that the weighted objective reduces to the
  current one when all weights are equal.

**Verify in one run:** log the pooling-weight ESS (`1/Σq²`) as a metric — it should jump
from ~10 to ~7,800. Then `theta_features` AUC across **3 seeds** against 0.740.

### 2. The GPU-free ceiling suite — *run this today, it may cap everything else*

Three CPU computations on the 606 train repertoires, reusing `baseline.py::clonotype_keys`
and `SequenceDataset.sampling_probabilities`:

- **(a) Sampling ceiling.** Take `BurdenScoreClassifier.fit`'s per-repertoire hit-count
  vector; Monte-Carlo the oracle score under (bag size B) × (abundance-weighted vs uniform)
  and report AUC(B). Preliminary estimate: **~0.69 at B=8192, ~0.82 at 4×16384**. If that
  holds, 0.740 is already at the sampling ceiling and the only lever on the label branch is
  bag size or vocabulary restriction — which would redirect the whole programme.
- **(b) Rank vs φ.** Fit rank-T NMF/LDA over the ≥4-repertoire clonotype vocabulary, score
  with the *exact* in-batch objective (4 repertoires × 8192, `logsumexp_t(log θ + log β)`
  minus the batch partition) at T ∈ {8, 30, 90, 300}, plus an oracle per-clonotype table
  (T = ∞). Separates "rank is the ceiling" from "smooth φ is the ceiling" — i.e. it settles
  §2 and your point about smooth signal, without a GPU.
- **(c) Recompute the MI ceiling** under the abundance-weighted sampling distribution and
  the K=4 in-batch protocol, so it is comparable to `tm_gain`. The current 0.052 figure is
  already contradicted by a measured val gain of 0.159 and should stop being quoted.

**Cost:** CPU-hours, no model changes, no GPU.

### 3. Steps-per-repertoire-matched scaling at T=60 — *your point 1*

Tests the update-count hypothesis directly, which the log re-indexing above already
supports. Hold **steps-per-repertoire constant at ~600** across N ∈ {64, 128, 256, 606},
with `n_topics_signal: 20` / `n_topics_nonsignal: 40` (T=60 as you asked), everything else
from `config_overfit_free_*.yaml`.

- Set `n_epochs` per N so that `steps = 600 × N / 4`: N=64 → ~46 epochs, N=128 → ~100,
  N=256 → ~200, N=606 → ~150 (at 604 steps/epoch).
- Stop on a **`tm_gain` plateau**, not on `total_loss` — FINDINGS.md already documents
  early stopping being driven by reconstruction long after the branch of interest stopped
  moving.
- **Predicts (update-count):** gain ≈ 0.026 at every N, flat.
- **Predicts (some residual N-effect):** monotone decay surviving the budget match.
- **Free bonus:** T=60 at matched steps/rep is directly comparable to the existing T=30
  runs, giving the T-contrast the original sweep never had. (The only T-signal in the corpus
  today — `overfit_moretopics_full`, T=90, 0.0095 at 167 steps/rep vs `overfit_amortized_full`,
  T=30, 0.0078 at 454 steps/rep — is budget-adjusted ~3× in favour of more topics, but it
  is one confounded pair.)
- Memory is a non-issue: the `(R, S, T)` intermediate is 0.016 GB at T=30, 0.031 at T=60.

### 4. Reproduce v1 on S1/S2 — with the clamp fixed

Run the literal v1 config: `theta_mode="free"`, `theta_normalization="none"`,
`tm_likelihood_family="raw_bce"`, all entropy/usage/L1 coefficients 0, `lr 1e-3`, **T=8 as
4+4** (every synthetic run to date used 15), `latent_dim=70`, `vae_coef=0.005`. Gate the
guard at `airrtm_model.py:131-136` behind an explicit opt-in flag rather than removing it.

**Fix the dead gradient first** (`composite_loss.py:389-391`): replace `clamp` +
`binary_cross_entropy` with `binary_cross_entropy_with_logits` on a bounded score, or at
minimum log the fraction of sequences whose score falls outside `(0,1)` — if >50% are
clipped the loss is dead and the run says nothing.

**Read out:** `precision_at_k` / `signal_enrichment` against the planted motifs — v1's own
metric — not `label_acc`.

### 5. Free per-clonotype φ over the public vocabulary — *the direct test of §2*

Gives the model the per-item parameter a classical topic model's β has, on the label-free
public subset that provably contains all Fisher-selectable clonotypes.

- Build the vocabulary (CDR3+V+J seen in ≥4 **train** repertoires — fitting on all 748 would
  leak the test split) in `cli/preprocess_sequences.py`. `BurdenScoreClassifier.fit` already
  computes the `total_counts` array this needs; `clonotype_keys` currently returns
  `np.unique(keys)` and needs a variant returning per-row keys.
- Carry `clonotype_ids` through `utils/sequence_dataset.py` and `_Columns`.
- `self.phi_table = nn.Embedding(V+1, n_topics)` with an OOV bucket for private clonotypes,
  and `seq_topic_logits_ST = latent_space_to_topic_proportions_layer(...) + phi_table(ids)`
  in `AIRRTM_Model.forward` — additive, so the smooth path stays live and the two can be
  ablated against each other.

**Expected:** `tm_gain` up 10–50×; AUC ≥ 0.85 if topic structure adds anything over Fisher,
≈0.874 if it is just Fisher in a trenchcoat. Either outcome is worth having.

**Read out in one run:** `tm_gain` over the first 10 epochs, then `theta_features` AUC
(3 seeds) against 0.874 on the unchanged 152 test repertoires.

### 6. The Θ parameterisation 2×2 — *after experiment 1*

`theta_normalization ∈ {softmax, none}` × `entropy pair ∈ {on, off}`, on the flagship recipe
with experiment 1's fix already in. Replace the entropy terms with v1's L2
(`topic_proportions_l2_coef = 1e-4`) in the "off" arms. Three seeds per cell.

## Verification

- **Experiment 1:** pooling-weight ESS metric jumps ~3 orders of magnitude;
  `theta_features.roc_auc` across 3 seeds vs 0.740. Also re-run `pytest tests/` (86 tests) —
  the weighting change touches paths with existing regression coverage.
- **Experiment 2:** self-verifying; output is three curves, no training run.
- **Experiment 3:** fit `tm_gain` vs N at matched steps-per-repertoire; read from
  `history.json` at the plateau, not the last logged line.
- **Experiments 4:** `precision_at_k` / `signal_enrichment` vs S1/S2 planted sequences, plus
  the fraction-clipped diagnostic.
- **Experiments 5, 6:** `evaluate-model` → `report.json` → `theta_features.roc_auc` and
  `theta_features_cv.roc_auc_mean` on the unchanged 152-repertoire split; experiment 5
  additionally reports `signal_enrichment.pr_auc_over_chance` against the Fisher list on the
  70 positive test repertoires (chance = 1.0×; every checkpoint to date is 0.94–1.11×).
- **Standing rules:** training-loop `label_acc` does not predict held-out AUC in direction or
  magnitude — always pull a checkpoint and evaluate. And nothing under ~0.10 AUC counts from
  a single run.
